OVERVIEW

First: forget the “permanent vs. temporary” rule.

SER and ESTAR do not mean “permanent” and “temporary.” The choice depends on what you want to express: identity, classification, location, a state, an event, an evaluation, and other functions. Context can also change how an adjective is interpreted. Start from the function and meaning you need instead.

Puebla está en México.

Hoy es martes.

El árbol está muerto.

Core Uses

Start by identifying the function you want to express. These are the most productive patterns.

SER

Identity and classification
Soy Juan. / Es mi hermana.
Profession
Soy maestro. / Ana es doctora.
Origin and nationality
Soy de México. / Soy mexicano.
Characteristics and descriptions
La casa es azul. / Juan es tranquilo.
Possession
La casa es mía. / Ese coche es de Ana.
Material
La mesa es de madera.
Time and date
Son las tres. / Hoy es martes.
Time and location of events
La clase es mañana. / El concierto es a las ocho. / La fiesta es en mi casa.

ESTAR

Location of people and things
Estoy en casa. / Las llaves están en la mesa. / Puebla está en México.
Physical and emotional states
Estoy cansado. / Está feliz. / Está enferma.
Resulting states
La puerta está cerrada. / El vaso está roto. / El árbol está muerto.
Actions in progress
Estoy trabajando. / Estamos comiendo.

LEARN

Events vs. Locations

English normally uses “to be” in both cases. In Spanish, use SER when an event takes place. Use ESTAR when a person, thing, or place is located.

SER An event takes place

La fiesta es en mi casa.
The party takes place at my house.

ESTAR Something is located

Mi casa está en Puebla.
My house is in Puebla.

SER An event takes place

El concierto es en el teatro.
The concert takes place at the theater.

ESTAR Something is located

El teatro está en el centro.
The theater is downtown.

Adjectives That Change Meaning

With some adjectives, changing SER to ESTAR changes the interpretation of the adjective itself. Learn these combinations in context rather than trying to predict every meaning from a formula.

aburrido

SER

Es aburrido. — He/it is boring.

ESTAR

Está aburrido. — He is bored.

listo

SER

Es listo. — He is clever.

ESTAR

Está listo. — He is ready.

seguro

SER

Es seguro. — It/he is safe or reliable.

ESTAR

Está seguro. — He/she is sure.

verde

SER

Es verde. — It is green.

ESTAR

Está verde. — It is unripe / not ready.

rico

SER

Es rico. — He/she is rich.

ESTAR

Está rico. — It tastes good / is delicious.

vivo

SER

Es vivo. — He/she is clever or shrewd.

ESTAR

Está vivo. — He/she is alive.

LEARN

Same Adjective, Different Perspective

Sometimes the adjective keeps its basic meaning, but the verb changes how the speaker presents the description. SER commonly characterizes the subject; ESTAR can present an evaluation grounded in a particular situation or experience.

Characterizing with SER

La película es aburrida.
The movie is characterized as boring.

Evaluating with ESTAR

La película está aburrida.
The speaker evaluates the movie or experience in this situation.

Characterizing with SER

El restaurante es bueno.
The restaurant is characterized positively.

Evaluating with ESTAR

El restaurante está muy bueno.
The speaker evaluates a particular experience positively.

Characterizing with SER

Ana es guapa.
Ana is characterized as attractive.

Evaluating with ESTAR

Ana está guapísima hoy.
The speaker evaluates how Ana looks in this situation.

LEARN

Bueno, Malo, Bien & Mal

These four words are extremely common, but they do not belong to the same grammatical category. BUENO/A and MALO/A are adjectives: they describe or evaluate nouns and change for gender and number. BIEN and MAL are adverbs: they do not change form.

BUENO/A adjectiveMALO/A adjectiveBIEN adverbMAL adverb

ser bueno/a

To be a good person, have good qualities, or be good at something.
Es buena maestra. / Mi hermano es bueno para cocinar.

ser malo/a

To be a bad person, have poor qualities, or be bad at something.
Es malo para las matemáticas. / La película es mala.

estar bueno/a

For food: to taste good. El mole está bueno.
For a person, it can describe physical attractiveness and may sound sexual; guapo/a is more neutral.

estar malo/a

For food or products: to be spoiled or in bad condition. La leche está mala.
For illness, está enfermo/a is the clearer neutral choice in Mexico.

estar bien

To be okay/well, acceptable, or correct.
Estoy bien. / La comida está bien. / Está bien así.

estar mal

To be unwell, wrong, incorrect, or in a bad situation.
Estoy mal del estómago. / La respuesta está mal. / La situación está mal.

Important: BIEN and MAL are not used with SER. Forms such as *es bien and *es mal do not exist in standard Spanish. Use BUENO/A or MALO/A instead: Es bueno. / Es malo.

Bien and mal are not simply the “ESTAR versions” of bueno and malo. They also modify actions and other verbs: Trabaja bien. / Cocina mal. / Me siento bien. / Salió mal.

REFERENCE

Quick Reference

Ask what the sentence is doing, not whether something is permanent or temporary.

SER

Identity and classification · profession · origin and nationality · characteristics and descriptions · possession · material · time and date · time and location of events.

ESTAR

Location of people and things · physical and emotional states · resulting states · actions in progress.

Events and locations

La fiesta es en mi casa. — An event takes place.
Mi casa está en Puebla. — A place is located.

Meaning pairs SER / ESTAR

aburrido: boring / bored
listo: clever / ready
seguro: safe or reliable / sure
verde: green / unripe or not ready
rico: rich / tastes good or delicious
vivo: clever or shrewd / alive

Perspective

SER commonly characterizes the subject. ESTAR can present an evaluation grounded in a particular situation or experience. El restaurante es bueno. / El restaurante está muy bueno.

Bueno, malo, bien and mal

Bueno/a and malo/a are adjectives and change for gender and number. Bien and mal are adverbs and do not change form. Do not use bien or mal with SER in these patterns. Es bueno. / Es malo. / Estoy bien. / La respuesta está mal. Bien and mal also modify other verbs: Trabaja bien. / Cocina mal.

Usage notes

El mole está bueno: the food tastes good. La leche está mala: the milk is in bad condition. For illness, está enfermo/a is the clearer neutral choice in Mexico. For a person, estar bueno/a can describe physical attractiveness and may sound sexual; guapo/a is more neutral.

Scope

These are the most useful patterns for choosing between SER and ESTAR. Spanish allows additional uses and nuances that are best learned gradually and in context. The examples prioritize contemporary Mexican Spanish; meanings and levels of directness can vary by region and context.
